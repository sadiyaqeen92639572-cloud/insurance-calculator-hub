COBRA vs. ACA Marketplace: Which Is Cheaper?

Losing a job-based health plan gives you two paths: keep it via COBRA, or shop the Marketplace. Here's how to actually decide.

Written & fact-checked by the CoverFormula editorial team · Last reviewed 2026-07-29

Why This Decision Catches People Off Guard

When you lose job-based coverage, your former employer is required to offer COBRA continuation — but the sticker shock is real, because you suddenly pay both your own payroll deduction and the portion your employer used to cover, plus a 2% administration fee. What most people don't realize is that losing job-based coverage also triggers a Special Enrollment Period for the ACA Marketplace, meaning COBRA is never your only option.

The Core Tradeoff

COBRA keeps your exact same plan, doctors, and network — no new deductible, no re-shopping for coverage. The ACA Marketplace usually costs less on paper because of income-based subsidies, but it may mean a new plan, a new network, and a fresh deductible.

  • COBRA wins if you're mid-treatment with specific providers you don't want to lose, or if your income is high enough that Marketplace subsidies would be minimal anyway.
  • Marketplace usually wins on pure cost if your household income dropped along with your job — lower income means a larger subsidy against the benchmark Silver plan.

The 60-Day Window Strategy

You have 60 days from your qualifying event (or from the COBRA election notice, whichever is later) to elect COBRA — and that election is retroactive to your coverage-loss date. A common approach: wait through the window without paying anything. If a medical bill or claim comes in, elect COBRA retroactively and it covers the gap. If nothing comes up, let the window close and enroll in a Marketplace plan through your Special Enrollment Period instead. This isn't a loophole — it's how the two systems are designed to interact.

Run Both Numbers Before Deciding

The only way to know which option is actually cheaper for your situation is to calculate both: your true COBRA premium (payroll deduction + employer contribution + 2% fee) and your subsidized Marketplace premium (based on household income, size, and state).
