Do I Need an Umbrella Policy? 5 Warning Signs

Your auto and home policies have limits — here's how to tell if a lawsuit could blow past them.

Written & fact-checked by the CoverFormula editorial team · Last reviewed 2026-07-29

What Umbrella Insurance Actually Does

Personal umbrella insurance is extra liability coverage that kicks in once your auto or homeowners liability limits are exhausted — protecting your savings, home equity, and future income from a judgment that exceeds those limits. It's inexpensive relative to the coverage it provides, but most people don't think about it until one of these five factors applies to them.

1. You Have a Teen Driver

New drivers statistically get into more accidents, and a serious at-fault crash can generate a liability claim well beyond typical auto policy limits (often $250k-$300k per person). If your household added a teen driver, your liability exposure just went up significantly.

2. You Have a Pool, Trampoline, or Similar Attractive Nuisance

Pools, trampolines, and other features that draw in neighborhood kids create elevated premises-liability risk. An injury on your property — even to someone who wasn't invited — can result in a lawsuit that tests your homeowners liability limit.

3. You Own Rental Property

Landlord liability is broader than most people expect — a tenant or their guest injured on your rental property can sue you personally, and a standard landlord policy's liability limit may not be enough on its own.

4. You Own a Dog With Bite-Claim History (by Breed or Individual)

Dog bite claims are one of the most common sources of homeowners liability payouts. Certain breeds carry statistically higher claim rates, and insurers price (or exclude) coverage accordingly — an umbrella policy adds a buffer on top.

5. Your Net Worth Exceeds Your Current Liability Limits

This is the big one: the standard industry guideline is to carry umbrella coverage equal to at least your total net worth, rounded up to the next $1,000,000. If a lawsuit judgment can reach your savings, investments, and home equity beyond what your auto/home policies cover, that gap is exactly what an umbrella policy closes. Renters with meaningful savings or future earning potential need this too — homeownership isn't the deciding factor, net worth is.
